Structures

My work in structures spans a wide range of analysis and design problems, from conventional structural systems governed by standard code provisions to complex structures requiring advanced modeling and nonlinear dynamic analysis. The common thread is an emphasis on understanding governing mechanisms, selecting an appropriate level of analytical fidelity, and interpreting results in a way that directly informs engineering decisions.

Focus Areas

Structural design: conventional to advanced analysis

Design and verification of structural systems from code-based analysis to advanced simulation when demands are governed by nonlinear response, large deformations, contact/impact, or dynamic effects. Emphasis on selecting the right level of analytical fidelity for the decision and keeping assumptions auditable.

Performance-based assessment and retrofit reasoning

Model-driven evaluation of existing structures to identify governing mechanisms, bound uncertainties, and compare retrofit alternatives using decision-relevant performance measures and traceable assumptions.

The goal is not “more modeling.” It’s making modeling reliable enough that decisions can be defended.

Methods & Tools

Structural problems are approached using a combination of engineering judgment, simplified bounding analyses, and detailed numerical simulation as appropriate for the decision being supported. In addition to conventional analysis and design workflows, I’ve developed pre- and post-processing tools and API-driven automation to support repeatable modeling, reduce manual error, and improve traceability of results.

Structural analysis and design platforms

Conventional modeling and design workflows using CSI software (SAP2000, ETABS, SAFE) for structural systems, load combinations, design checks, and results interpretation.

Advanced finite element analysis

High-fidelity nonlinear simulation using advanced finite element tools (e.g., Abaqus, LS-DYNA) for problems involving nonlinear material behavior, contact/impact, large deformation, and nonlinear dynamics.

Automation and model tooling

Development of pre-/post-processors and software API–based workflows to support parametric studies, model generation, result extraction, quality checks, and consistent reporting.

Structural Analysis and Design

Port and Maritime Structures

Analysis and design of port and waterfront structures—including piers, wharves, seawalls, bulkheads, and dolphin systems—using applicable waterfront design standards such as ASCE 61, UFC 4‑152, and MOTEMS. Project work may incorporate Port of Long Beach Wharf Design Criteria (POLB WDC) for wharf‑specific load combinations, soil–structure interaction requirements, and seismic performance levels. Nonlinear methods (including FEMA/ATC‑style pushover analysis) are applied where needed to evaluate performance and support strengthening and retrofit decision‑making.

Special Structures

Projects involving advanced analytical approaches for offshore and nearshore systems, including nonlinear response evaluation, nonlinear dynamics, interaction effects, and specialized loading such as impact, hydrodynamic, and offshore-related extreme events. Example work includes offshore wind turbine foundation analysis, submerged energy-harvesting structures, and offshore anchor system design (including suction caissons, drag anchors, plate anchors, and torpedo anchors). Experience includes participation as a team member in advanced analysis and design of anchor systems and 3D-printed structural concepts for offshore energy applications, using nonlinear material and geometric behavior with advanced meshing, and large-deformation simulations using both Lagrangian and Eulerian formulations. Design and verification have utilized international offshore standards including DNV offshore unit rules/standards, ABS offshore guidance, and relevant Eurocodes.

Port and Nearshore Structure Assessment and Load Rating

Assessment, load rating, and rehabilitation planning for existing port and nearshore structures—including piers, wharves, bulkheads, seawalls, and dolphins—with emphasis on operational/environmental demands, boundary conditions, and governing deterioration or capacity limits. Evaluation and retrofit approaches may use the same waterfront criteria (ASCE 61, UFC 4‑152, MOTEMS, and POLB WDC) framed for existing‑asset decision making, including demand‑capacity checks and comparison of strengthening alternatives. Nonlinear procedures (including FEMA/ATC‑based pushover methods) are applied when needed to capture displacement‑based behavior and evaluate retrofit options.

Rehabilitation of Large-Scale Infrastructure Systems

Evaluation and rehabilitation of large‑scale Navy and defense‑related waterfront infrastructure and heavy civil systems where boundary conditions and nonlinear response govern retrofit decisions. Work has included soil–structure interaction (SSI) modeling using hand calculations, simplified 2D approaches, and high‑fidelity 3D nonlinear models to capture foundation behavior, interaction effects, and demand sensitivity. Analyses have used Abaqus and LS‑DYNA with nonlinear material and geometric behavior, advanced meshing, and large‑deformation simulations (Lagrangian and Eulerian formulations as appropriate), supported by Python‑based pre‑ and post‑processing for repeatable model setup and results extraction. Applicable Navy criteria may include MIL-STD-1625 for safety/certification expectations for drydocking facilities and shipbuilding ways supporting U.S. Navy ships.
