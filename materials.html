Infrastructure Materials

My work in infrastructure materials focuses on cementitious systems where long-term performance is governed by coupled chemistry and transport. The emphasis is on model robustness, interpretable assumptions, and workflows that connect thermodynamics, reactive transport, and durability mechanisms. Much of this work has been developed in research environments and is oriented toward understanding governing mechanisms and sensitivity, rather than producing black-box outputs.

What I’m Known For

Reactive-transport modeling in cementitious materials

Coupling transport and chemical reactions to represent multi-species processes in porous media, including cement systems with complex chemistry and evolving pore solution conditions.

Work examples (in preparation): brief writeups with figures and notes.

Thermodynamic modeling for cement chemistry

Thermodynamic equilibrium modeling to represent chemical speciation, phase behavior, and binding mechanisms, and linking those outputs to durability-relevant transport processes.

Work examples (in preparation): model schematic, assumptions, and key outputs.

Corrosion and chloride ingress mechanisms

Modeling transport-driven deterioration relevant to reinforcement corrosion, including chloride ingress and simplified reactive-transport representations to capture binding and exposure sensitivity.

Work examples (in preparation): parameter sensitivity and exposure comparisons.

Hydration, heat, and mass transport coupling

Multiphysics representations that connect hydration reactions with heat and mass transport, supporting mechanistic interpretation of early-age behavior and environmental conditioning.

Work examples (in preparation): conceptual diagrams and representative results.

Model integration and repeatable studies

Structured modeling workflows that keep chemistry, transport, and boundary conditions consistent across cases, enabling controlled comparisons and clear sensitivity analysis. (Software details are covered separately under Engineering Software.)

Work examples (in preparation): workflow diagrams and study templates.

Model robustness and interpretability

A bias toward clarity: bounding cases, sensitivity runs, and transparent assumptions so outcomes can be trusted and communicated without “black-box” behavior.

Work examples (in preparation): validation checks and sensitivity patterns.
